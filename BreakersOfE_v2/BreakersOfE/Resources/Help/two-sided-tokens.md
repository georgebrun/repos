# Two-Sided Tokens
> Two tokens printed on one card: one row of its own, "Officer // Treasure".

Many tokens have another token printed on the back: Commander decks have had them since 2014, booster packs since Modern Horizons (2019), and Reality Fracture's and the Star Trek decks' tokens are two-sided. Scryfall lists each side as its own token and doesn't say which two are printed together (booster pairings vary, and decks differ), so you tell BoE. A token Scryfall already has as one double-faced card (Incubator // Phyrexian) is one card already and needs nothing.

## Adding a two-sided token
1. In **Edit → Pool → Collection → Tokens**, find the token in the pool (on top) and add it as usual: [Enter], [Shift+Enter], or **Add Non-Foil / Add Foil**.
2. The **Other Side** window lists the card pool's tokens of that release (all its token sets). Untick the release box to see every token; **Find** narrows the list.
3. Pick the token on the back and click **Add to Collection** (or double-click it). The card goes in as one row: **Officer // Treasure**, in the language and condition you chose, and a finish both sides come in.
4. No other side: **Single-Sided** ([Enter]). **Cancel** ([Esc]) adds nothing.

Tokens released before Dark Ascension (February 2012, the first two-sided tokens) don't ask. Adding more of a token you've paired before opens with the same other side picked. Adding several selected tokens at once doesn't ask: pair them afterwards.

## How a two-sided card shows
- It's one row, named with both sides in alphabetical order: **Officer // Treasure**, whichever side you picked first (a card has no front). It sorts under its first side's name, and searching either name finds it.
- It's a card like any other: its own quantity, finish, language, condition, Used and Available. Removing it removes the card; changing its finish, language or condition changes the card.
- The card panel and the card window show its first side with **Show Other Side** for the second.
- Single tokens of either side stay rows of their own: an Officer you have on its own is an **Officer** row.

## Decks
A deck holds a two-sided card as one token line, **Officer // Treasure**, and claims it from the card's row, like any card. At the playtest table it's one token you turn over.

## Making and taking apart two-sided cards
- Select a single token row and click **Two-Sided…** (or right-click → **Other Side…**), then pick the token on its back. **Pair** makes that many two-sided cards: the copies come off the token's row and, when you have free single copies of the back, off that row too (else the back comes from the card pool). Copies in decks stay as they are.
- Right-click a two-sided card → **Unpair** takes Qty of its free copies apart: each side goes back to a single row of its own.
- [Ctrl+Z] puts either one back.

## Sharing pairs
Right-click → **Export Two-Sided Pairs…** saves your two-sided cards as a CSV file (each side's name, set code and collector number, and how many). **Import Two-Sided Pairs…** reads one (yours or a friend's), lists the pairs you have free single copies of both sides of, and makes the cards when you say so. Check them against your cards: pairings can differ.

## Suggested Tokens and print slips
- **Suggested Tokens…** finds a token on either side of your two-sided cards, and on either face of a double-faced token. **Prefer** picks which you're offered first when you have the token both on its own and on a two-sided card (remembered).
- When one card of yours is both tokens a deck makes, it says *one card with …*: that card is used for both, one side at a time.
- Print slips of a two-sided card print both sides (Side 1 of 2, Side 2 of 2); tick **Print one side only** for just the first.

## From earlier versions
Earlier versions kept the two sides as two rows linked together. The first time this version starts, each linked pair becomes a two-sided card of its own: its copies come off the two side rows, and decks that held either side hold the card instead. The collection and the decks it changes are backed up first (in your Backups folder, "Before two-sided cards …"), and it says what it did.
